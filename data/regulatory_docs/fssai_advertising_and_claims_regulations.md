# FSSAI Food Safety and Standards (Advertising and Claims) Regulations

## 1. General Principles of Food Advertising
Under Regulation 4 of the FSSAI (Advertising and Claims) Regulations:
- Advertisements shall not deceive or mislead consumers as to the nature, substance, quality, or quantity of the food.
- Advertisements shall not undermine the importance of a healthy lifestyle or disparage clean whole foods.
- Advertisements shall not present words, pictures, or graphics that imply nutritional properties or benefits that the product does not possess.

## 2. Specific Nutrient Content Claims

### A. Sugar Claims
- **"Sugar-Free" / "Zero Sugar":**
  - A food may be described as "Sugar-Free" or "Zero Sugar" only if the product contains no more than **0.5 g of sugars per 100 g** (for solid foods) or **0.5 g per 100 ml** (for liquid foods).
- **"No Added Sugar":**
  - May be made only if no sugar, honey, molasses, or ingredients containing sugars (e.g., fruit juice concentrates, maltodextrin, invert sugar syrup, liquid glucose, corn syrup) have been added during manufacture.
  - If the food contains naturally occurring sugars (such as lactose in milk or fructose in fruit), the label **MUST prominently state**: *"Contains naturally occurring sugars"*.
  - Claiming "No Added Sugar" while adding high-glycemic starches like **Maltodextrin** or **Liquid Glucose** is considered deceptive under Schedule II, Section 4.

### B. Grain & Flour Claims ("Whole Wheat" / "Atta")
- **"100% Whole Wheat" / "100% Atta":**
  - The grain flour component of the product must be **100% Whole Wheat Flour (Atta)** with zero refined wheat flour (Maida).
- **"Whole Wheat Biscuits / Bread":**
  - If a product claims to be a "Whole Wheat" or "Atta" product on the front of the pack, the total flour content must comprise **at least 60% Whole Wheat Flour (Atta)** by weight.
  - If Whole Wheat Flour is less than 60% (e.g., 20% Atta and 80% Maida), naming or advertising the product as "Atta Cookie" or "Whole Wheat Snack" without prominently disclosing the percentage of Maida on the front of the pack is a misleading violation under Section 7.

### C. Protein Claims
- **"Source of Protein":**
  - Must provide at least **10% of the Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA)** of protein per 100 g for solids (minimum 5 g protein per 100 g).
- **"High Protein":**
  - Must provide at least **20% of the RDA** of protein per 100 g for solids (minimum 10 g to 12 g protein per 100 g).
  - Products marketing themselves as "Protein Bars" or "Protein Cereals" that derive more than 40% of their total energy from free sugars or saturated fats are deemed deceptive if they conceal the net negative nutritional balance.

### D. Fat & Trans Fat Claims
- **"Trans Fat Free":**
  - The food must contain less than **0.2 g of trans fats per 100 g** or 100 ml.
  - Saturated fat content must not exceed 1.5 g per 100 g.
- **Hydrogenated Vegetable Oils:**
  - If partially hydrogenated vegetable oils or palm olein are used, the product cannot claim "Heart Healthy" or "Light".

### E. "Natural" and "Fresh" Claims
- The word **"Natural"** shall only be used for single foods to which nothing has been added and which have been subjected only to such processing as to render them suitable for human consumption (e.g., washing, peeling, freezing).
- Composite processed foods containing synthetic additives, artificial flavors, emulsifiers, or preservatives (e.g., INS 211, INS 322) are **legally prohibited** from using the term "Natural" or "100% Natural" on the principal display panel.

## 3. Labeling and Front-of-Pack Disclosures (FSSAI 2020 Amendment)
- **Quantitative Ingredient Declaration (QUID):** When an ingredient is highlighted on the front of the packaging (e.g., "Almond & Honey Granola"), the exact percentage of that ingredient must be specified in the ingredients list (e.g., "Almonds 2%").
- Highlighted ingredients present at negligible quantities (< 5%) used to disguise base ingredients (like Palm Oil or Refined Flour) violate the FSSAI Fair Trade Practice code.
