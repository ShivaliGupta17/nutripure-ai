# World Health Organization (WHO) & International Food Safety Standards

## 1. WHO Guidelines on Sugars and Sweeteners

### Free Sugars Threshold
- The World Health Organization (WHO) strongly recommends reducing the intake of **free sugars to less than 10% of total energy intake**, with a conditional recommendation to reduce it to **below 5% (approximately 25 grams or 6 teaspoons per day)** for additional health benefits.
- **Hidden & Disguised Sugars:** Free sugars include monosaccharides and disaccharides added to foods and beverages by the manufacturer, cook, or consumer, plus sugars naturally present in honey, syrups, fruit juices, and fruit juice concentrates.
- **High-Glycemic Industrial Carbohydrates:** Ingredients such as **Maltodextrin** have a Glycemic Index (GI) of 110 to 185 (higher than table sugar at 65). When used as bulking agents, they spike blood glucose rapidly and are physiologically equivalent to free sugars.

### Non-Sugar Sweeteners (NSS) - WHO 2023 Guideline
- WHO advises against the use of Non-Sugar Sweeteners (NSS) for weight control or reducing the risk of noncommunicable diseases (NCDs).
- **Common NSS:** Acesulfame K (INS 950), Aspartame (INS 951), Advantame, Cyclamates, Neotame, Saccharin (INS 954), Sucralose (INS 955), and Stevia derivatives (INS 960).
- **Long-term Risks:** Long-term consumption of NSS is linked with increased risk of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and mortality in adults.
- **Vulnerable Populations:** Not recommended for children, pregnant women, or individuals with phenylketonuria (in the case of Aspartame).

## 2. NOVA Classification of Food Processing (Universally Adopted by WHO/FAO)

### Group 1: Unprocessed or Minimally Processed Foods
- Whole foods obtained directly from nature: fresh/frozen fruits, vegetables, whole grains (brown rice, whole wheat grain, rolled oats), nuts, eggs, fresh milk, and meats.
- Processing involves physical methods like cleaning, grading, grinding, drying, or pasteurization without adding substances like sugar, oils, or industrial chemicals.

### Group 2: Processed Culinary Ingredients
- Substances obtained from Group 1 foods or nature by pressing, refining, or grinding (e.g., cold-pressed vegetable oils, butter, lard, table sugar, salt, and vinegar).
- Rarely consumed alone; used to prepare and cook Group 1 foods.

### Group 3: Processed Foods
- Simple industrial products made by adding Group 2 ingredients (salt, sugar, oil) to Group 1 foods to preserve or enhance flavor (e.g., canned vegetables in brine, freshly baked artisan bread, salted nuts, cheeses).
- Typically contain 2 to 3 ingredients.

### Group 4: Ultra-Processed Food Formulations (UPF)
- Industrial formulations typically made from 5 or more ingredients not commonly found in culinary preparations.
- **Hallmarks of UPF:**
  - Hydrolyzed proteins, soya protein isolate, whey isolate, maltodextrin, inverted sugar.
  - Cosmetic additives: flavor enhancers (INS 621 / MSG), artificial food colors (Tartrazine INS 102, Caramel IV INS 150d), emulsifiers (Carrageenan INS 407, Polysorbate 80 INS 433), anti-foaming agents, and artificial sweeteners.
- **Clinical Association:** UPF consumption is directly correlated with higher incidence of obesity, metabolic syndrome, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and colorectal cancer.

## 3. Specific Additive Hazards & Toxicity Alerts

### Emulsifiers & Gut Microbiota Disruption
- **Carrageenan (INS 407):** Seaweed extract commonly used in dairy, ice creams, and plant milks. Degraded carrageenan causes intestinal inflammation, ulcerations, and disrupts the gut mucosal barrier.
- **Carboxymethyl Cellulose (CMC / INS 466) & Polysorbate 80 (INS 433):** Shown in peer-reviewed clinical studies to erode the mucus layer of the colon, promoting chronic inflammation and metabolic syndrome.

### Preservatives & Chemical Interactions
- **Sodium Benzoate (INS 211):** When combined with Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid / INS 300) in acidic drinks, it can react to form **Benzene**, a known Group 1 human carcinogen.
- **Sulphur Dioxide & Sulphites (INS 220 - INS 228):** Can trigger severe bronchospasms in asthmatic consumers.
